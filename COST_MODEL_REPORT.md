# COST_MODEL_REPORT

Generated by `scripts/run_cost_model_report.py` — Work Package 7.

**No strategy profit or loss figure appears in this report.** It establishes
which side of the market our prices sit on, what a round trip costs, and how
that compares with the size of the stops the baseline actually produces.

## 1. The series is a BID series (measured)

The source does not say which side it quotes. The user's Dukascopy reference
files do, so the question is settled by comparison rather than assumption:

| Reference file | Side | Bars | Mean (ours − reference) | Median |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `XAU-USD_1Hour_ASK_2013-01-01_to_2013-02-01_Etc` | ASK | 525 | -0.4198 | -0.4130 |
| `XAU-USD_1Hour_BID_2013-02-02_to_2013-03-02_Etc` | BID | 457 | +0.0350 | +0.0400 |
| `XAU-USD_1Hour_BID_2013-07-01_to_2013-07-31_Etc` | BID | 524 | +0.1184 | +0.0995 |

Our prices sit ~$0.42 below the ask and within a few cents of the bid. They
are bid quotes.

**Why this decides the cost model.** Every level in this project — entries,
stops, targets, PDH/PDL, Asia high/low — is derived from this series and is
therefore in bid terms. So:

| Leg | Transacts at | Pays spread? |
|---|---|---|
| Long entry (buy) | ask | **yes** |
| Long exit (sell) | bid | no |
| Short entry (sell) | bid | no |
| Short exit (buy) | ask | **yes** |

Exactly one leg of every round trip pays the spread — never two, never none.
Assuming both legs pay would double the cost; assuming neither would delete
it, and no summary statistic would show which mistake had been made.

## 2. What the spread actually was

From the one month where we hold the ask side (525 hourly bars, January 2013):

median **$0.413/oz** · mean $0.420 · 25th pct $0.370 · 75th pct $0.457 · 95th pct $0.566

London window (07–10 UTC) median $0.397 · New York window (13–16 UTC) median $0.400.

Worth noting: our trading hours are **not** cheaper than the rest of the day.
A common assumption is that kill-zone hours bring tighter spreads; in this
sample they did not.

This is 2013 and an ECN-style broker — a wide-spread era for gold. It is
kept as its own labelled scenario rather than used as the baseline.

## 4. How big are the stops? (the finding that changed the baseline)

Measured across the 149 tradeable baseline setups from Work Package 6:

| | SL Variant A (sweep wick) | SL Variant B (broken swing) |
|---|---|---|
| Setups producing a valid order | **149 / 149** | 113 / 149 |
| Median stop | $3.85 | $0.81 |
| 10th percentile | $1.69 | $0.14 |
| 90th percentile | $6.76 | $2.96 |
| Stops under $0.50 | 0% | 38% |

For scale: the median FVG is $0.92 wide and median ATR(14) on the 15m series is $1.61.

**Why Variant B collapses.** The eligible FVG is created by the very
displacement candle that broke the reference swing, so the gap sits directly
on top of that swing. The entry — the gap's near edge — often lands almost
exactly on the stop, and in 24% of cases beyond it, which cannot be executed
at all. This is a mechanical consequence of combining two pre-registered rules,
and carries no information about whether the strategy is profitable.

## 5. Cost as a share of one R

This is the number that matters. A spread is not expensive or cheap in the
abstract — only relative to the risk being taken.

| Profile | @ Variant A median stop | @ Variant B median stop |
|---|---|---|
| `standard` | 10.4% | 49.5% |
| `standard_x2` | 20.8% | 98.9% |
| `raw_ecn` | 8.3% | 39.6% |
| `raw_ecn_x2` | 16.6% | 79.1% |
| `measured_2013` | 13.5% | 64.3% |
| `measured_2013_x2` | 27.0% | 128.6% |

And the same thing expressed as the hurdle a fixed 1:2 system has to clear —
the win rate required merely to break even, against **33.3%** with no costs:

| Profile | Variant A | Variant B |
|---|---|---|
| `standard` | 36.5% | 47.8% |
| `standard_x2` | 39.6% | 61.3% |
| `raw_ecn` | 35.8% | 44.7% |
| `raw_ecn_x2` | 38.2% | 55.2% |

Under Variant B a 1:2 system must win roughly half its trades just to stand
still. A test set up that way would almost certainly return "no edge" — but
the result would be uninformative, because it could not distinguish a weak
signal from a stop that was simply too tight to survive the spread.

Both variants are therefore now co-primary baselines, declared before any
backtest, run together and reported together. See the WP7 amendment in
`PREREGISTRATION.md`.

## 7. Verdict

The cost model is measured where measurement was possible (which side the
series quotes, and what the spread was in the one month we can check) and
clearly labelled as assumption everywhere else. It is ready for Work Package 8.

**Carried forward:** with a $0.30 spread, Variant A costs ~10% of R per trade
and Variant B ~49%. Costs are not a rounding error for this strategy; at the
tighter stop they are the dominant term.
