Generated by `scripts/run_engine_selftest.py` — Work Package 5.

## 1. Bar timestamp convention (verified, not assumed)

The source labels bars by **open time**: a bar labelled `T` covers
`[T, T+duration)` and is unknowable until `T+duration`. Verified by
aggregating m15 bars into h1 bars in both directions:

| Hypothesis | h1 bars matching |
|---|---|
| Timestamp = bar OPEN time | 2992 / 2992 |
| Timestamp = bar CLOSE time | 0 / 2992 |

Had the close-time reading been assumed, every decision in the project
would have carried a silent 15-minute lookahead.

## 2. Higher-timeframe bars

Baseline D1/H4 are rebuilt from m15 on the **17:00 New York** trading day
(PREREGISTRATION.md §2), DST-aware. The broker's native bars roll at 00:00
EET/EEST instead, which coincides with 17:00 NY most of the year but not
in the weeks where US and EU daylight-saving dates differ.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| m15 bars | 230,400 |
| D1 bars, 17:00-NY (ours) | 2,532 — from 2012-05-14 |
| D1 bars, broker native | 2,400 — from 2012-11-13 |
| H4 bars, 17:00-NY (ours) | 15,107 |
| Days where both definitions align | 2,242 |
| Days where they differ (DST mismatch) | 159 |
| **Max OHLC difference on aligned days** | **0.0000** |

A maximum difference of exactly zero across all aligned days is a strong
independent cross-check: our daily bars, rebuilt from 15-minute data via
the WP4 EET/EEST conversion, reproduce the broker's own daily bars
exactly. That validates the timezone conversion, the price scaling, the
m15 series and this aggregation in a single comparison.

Rebuilding also recovers roughly six months of development history the
native daily series does not cover.

## 4. Gap handling on real bars

Confirmed by user 2026-08-25 (WP5 Q2 = conservative): a gap through the
stop is taken in full at the bar open; a gap through the target is still
booked at the target. Costs of ambiguity fall on us in both directions.

## 6. Equity accounting

Fixed risk on **initial** equity, no compounding (user decision, WP5 Q4),
so the equity curve stays a faithful rescaling of the R sequence.

| Risk/trade | Final equity (start $100,000) | Max drawdown | Account ruined |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.25% | $22,765 | $-90,902 | no |
| 0.50% | $-54,470 | $-181,803 | yes — 2014-09-22 |
| 1.00% | $-208,940 | $-363,607 | yes — 2013-04-16 |

A non-compounding curve keeps subtracting the same cash risk after the
account would really have been wiped out, so the final figure can be
negative. That is why ruin is reported as its own column rather than
left to be inferred from a balance. These are the numbers an edge-free
system produces; they are here to show the accounting works, nothing more.

## 7. Verdict

The engine reproduces every hand-computed synthetic answer, refuses to
fill an order on the bar that created it, never returns more than the
planned target, takes real gap losses in full, and produces breakeven
results from information-free entries. **Proceed to Work Package 6
(core features).**

Not yet built, by design: transaction costs (WP7 — the engine has the
interface and a zero-cost placeholder only) and every piece of strategy
